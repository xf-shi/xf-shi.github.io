Course Overview

This course develops the stochastic-analysis tools used in modern mathematical finance, with an emphasis on derivative pricing and hedging.

Topics include discrete- and continuous-time models, options and the Greeks, replicating portfolios, interest-rate derivatives, foreign-exchange models, stochastic-volatility models, and related applications.

Course delivery. The course is delivered in person and attendance is expected. Video recordings will not be available. Any change in the mode of delivery will be announced on Quercus.

Prerequisites

Statistics, calculus-based probability, stochastic calculus, ODEs, and PDEs.

Enrollment and auditing

Students must be formally enrolled in the course in order to attend. Students who are not enrolled may not attend or audit lectures or tutorials without prior written permission from the instructor. Enrollment or waitlist status does not by itself constitute permission to attend.

References

  • Steven Shreve, Stochastic Calculus for Finance II: Continuous-Time Models.
  • John Hull, Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives.

Quizzes

There will be two 60-minute quizzes, approximately evenly spaced throughout the semester. Quizzes will be completed in person at the start of Monday tutorial sessions. Specific coverage will be announced in advance and will generally consist of material from the previous three lectures.

Projects

There will be two individual projects. Each project will require students to understand the relevant theory, formulate an approach, implement numerical work in MATLAB, R, or Python, interpret the results, and submit a formal report of no more than 9 pages, excluding references and the contribution sheet.

Reports should be written in proper academic English and should include an abstract, introduction, model setup, methodology/theory, and discussion or interpretation of results. Presentation quality, including figures and tables, will form part of the assessment.

Course Policies

Late Project Reports

Late homework and project reports will be penalized by 10 percentage points of the maximum grade for each started 24-hour period that the submission is late.

Missed Quizzes and Exams

If you miss a quiz or exam for a legitimate reason, you must contact the instructor within 24 hours of the missed assessment and, whenever possible, in advance.

If the absence is accepted as legitimate, the instructor may arrange a verbal makeup assessment of no more than one hour. The makeup will assess the same course material but will use different questions.

Regrading

Regrading is intended to correct marking or calculation errors. Requests must clearly identify the specific issue and must be sent by email to both the instructor and the TA within 48 hours after the work is returned. If a remark is requested, the entire submission may be re-evaluated and the final grade may increase, decrease, or remain unchanged.

Collaboration

Discussion of homework with classmates is encouraged, but submitted work must be written independently. Students may not copy another student's solution, use solutions from previous years, or use solutions obtained from external sources.

Academic Integrity

Academic integrity is fundamental to learning and scholarship at the University of Toronto. Suspected cases of academic misconduct will be handled under the University's Code of Behaviour on Academic Matters.

Copyright

Course materials, including notes, examples, and assessment questions, are the property of their respective authors. Materials may be downloaded for personal academic use but may not be redistributed without explicit permission.

Wellbeing and Support

Students experiencing academic or personal difficulties are encouraged to seek appropriate support early. University resources are available through U of T Student Mental Health services and other campus support offices.
